**Periode:** 10–23 November 2026 · **Zona waktu:** WIB / Asia/Jakarta (UTC+7)

14 hari, dua slot per hari: **08.30 WIB** untuk percakapan ringan dan ide singkat; **19.30 WIB** untuk produk, edukasi, thread, atau artikel. Slot ini merupakan titik awal eksperimen, bukan hasil analytics akun. Semua copy publik di bawah memakai bahasa Inggris.

## Cara menggunakan

- Salin hanya isi blok `text` untuk posting. Catatan visual berada di luar copy.
- Thread: posting blok pertama sebagai tweet utama, lalu blok berikutnya sebagai balasan berurutan pada thread yang sama.
- Artikel: gunakan naskah A1/A2 di bagian akhir, lalu posting copy pengantarnya pada slot malam. Jika format artikel tidak tersedia di akun, pecah naskah menjadi thread sebelum dipublikasikan.

## A1 — A token schedule is a product decision

**Subtitle:** The release terms deserve as much attention as the allocation.

An allocation tells a recipient how many tokens have been assigned to them. It does not, by itself, tell them when those tokens become available or what they need to do next.

That second part is where the release schedule becomes a product decision.

### Start with the recipient's questions

A recipient should be able to find three things without reconstructing the terms from scattered messages: their allocation, the timing, and the claim process.

If there is a cliff, explain it. If the stream is cancelable, say so. If tokens become claimable over time, distinguish that from an automatic transfer into the recipient's wallet.

These are basic mechanics, but the language around them affects how people understand the product.

### Separate the states

Allocated, vested, claimable, and claimed are related words. They are not interchangeable.

An allocation describes the amount assigned. Vesting describes entitlement under a schedule. The currently claimable amount reflects what is available to claim, while claimed tokens have already been collected.

An interface that blurs those states can make an ordinary schedule feel confusing. A clearer explanation gives each state a name and a next step.

### Treat the dates as part of the interface

The start, any cliff, and the end define the shape of a vesting schedule. They should be presented with a clear timezone and enough context to understand their relationship.

A timeline can help, but it should support the actual terms. A decorative progress bar is not an explanation of what the recipient can claim.

Review the recipient, asset, amount, displayed fees, and timing together before creating a stream. Each field contributes to the resulting arrangement.

### Make cancellation explicit

Cancellation is a meaningful term, not a detail to leave for later.

In Raqio, only streams created with cancellation enabled can be cancelled. Cancellation preserves the recipient's vested entitlement and returns the unvested portion to the sender.

That distinction belongs in the explanation before the recipient relies on the schedule.

### What Raqio contributes

Raqio provides token locks, vesting streams, and Merkle airdrops with optional vesting. These are different release patterns that teams can use to structure allocations.

The product does not turn a schedule into a guarantee about market demand or future price. Its role is to make the release structure concrete: who receives the tokens, under what timing, and through which claim process.

A useful final check is simple: ask the recipient to explain the schedule back in one sentence.

If they can, the terms and the explanation are working together.

## A2 — Design airdrops around the recipient

**Subtitle:** The distribution workflow continues after the allocation list is finished.

An airdrop announcement often starts with a number: the size of the allocation, the number of recipients, or the campaign's total funding.

For a recipient, the experience starts with a different question: what do I do now?

Designing a useful distribution means answering that question all the way through the claim process.

### Explain eligibility without ambiguity

Tell recipients which wallet is eligible and how their allocation is identified.

In a Merkle distribution, the campaign commits its allocations through a root. A recipient uses a proof associated with their allocation to claim. That proof has to reach the recipient through a practical, understandable process.

A completed allocation file is not the same thing as completed recipient instructions.

### Decide what a successful claim means

Does claiming make the allocation immediately available, or does it create a vesting stream?

Raqio supports Merkle airdrops with optional vesting. That choice changes what recipients should expect after claiming.

If a stream is created, explain its timing and how later claims work. Avoid language that suggests the full allocation will immediately arrive in the wallet when the release terms say otherwise.

### Put the important terms before the action

Recipients should understand the relevant deadlines and release conditions before they start the wallet flow.

If the campaign has an expiry, explain it in a clear timezone. If a cliff applies, explain the condition. If vesting is used, distinguish the total allocation from the amount currently available.

These details are easier to follow when they are presented together, instead of spread across replies, screenshots, and support messages.

### Write the instructions as a sequence

Good instructions follow the recipient's path: identify the eligible wallet, obtain the proof, review the allocation and terms, submit the claim, and understand the result.

For a direct distribution, the result is different from a distribution that creates a stream. Show the relevant next step for each case.

You do not need a long document for every recipient. You need enough explanation for someone to complete the process without guessing.

### Keep the claims about the product precise

A distribution tool structures the movement and release of tokens. It does not establish the future value of those tokens or guarantee the success of the project distributing them.

Raqio's role is specific: token locks, vesting streams, and Merkle airdrops with optional vesting.

That scope is useful on its own. The content around it should be just as specific.

The best recipient experience is one where the announcement, instructions, interface, and release terms all describe the same thing.

**Lock. Vest. Distribute. Raqio.**
